# Independent quantum output evaluation

`qn_atlas.quantum.evaluation.evaluate_execution(execution, task, reference)` evaluates each typed prediction independently. The execution envelope supplies status and provenance, never authoritative accuracy. `evaluate_prediction` is the single-output entry point. The new profile defaults to trace-distance target 0.01 for state preparation and absolute observable target 0.05, with confidence 0.99. Existing classical task definitions and identifiers are unchanged.

The authoritative task encoding specifies `num_qubits`, least-significant-first `system_qubits`, fixed `postselect` ancilla bits, ordered `physical_basis_indices`, and `bit_order='qiskit_little_endian'`. Optional `classical_bit_to_qubit` explicitly maps measured classical bits. Every qubit must be assigned; no unspecified ancilla is silently traced out. Qiskit count strings display the highest classical bit on the left. Full-register statevectors/density matrices and raw counts are decoded by the evaluator. Conflicting prediction encodings, wrong dimensions, nonfinite arrays, zero states and zero-probability postselection are rejected. Complex arrays use `{real, imag}`. Density matrices must be Hermitian and positive semidefinite to numerical tolerance.

For normalized pure states, error is phase-invariant trace distance. The implementation uses aligned-vector subtraction to avoid catastrophic cancellation near equal states. Mixed-state trace distance is half the sum of absolute eigenvalues of the Hermitian density-matrix difference. Postselected outputs are normalized only within the declared physical subspace; the probability of that projection remains a separate resource statistic. Norm or phase recovery of the original ODE solution is not implied.

Computational-basis samples cannot identify state phase and therefore cannot evaluate general quantum state trace distance. For diagonal observables, decoded conditional samples give a mean and a finite-confidence interval. Two-outcome observables use affine-transformed Clopper–Pearson binomial intervals; more outcomes use Hoeffding's bounded-variable inequality. The postselection probability has its own Clopper–Pearson interval. Splitting the failure probability equally by Bonferroni gives joint coverage at least the requested confidence, under iid shots, fixed decoding and fixed postselection. This is not a guarantee under correlated hardware errors or adaptive selection of favorable circuits from these same shots. One survivor does not produce a zero-width interval. No survivors leaves the conditional mean unestimated. Insufficient interval precision prevents accuracy ranking.

The platform reference supplies `statevector`, `density_matrix`, `trajectory` or `observable_expectation`, together with `source`, `qualified`, `uncertainty` and its kind. `uncertainty_metric` must be `trace_distance` for state preparation or `absolute_expectation` for state expectation. Any task-supplied `task_id`, `scenario_id` and `timepoint` must match the reference exactly. A trajectory-norm uncertainty cannot be relabeled as normalized-state error. Qualification requires uncertainty at most epsilon/10; zero uncertainty is accepted only for an explicitly validated closed-form reference (`uncertainty_kind='closed_form_validated', validated=True`). Missing reference evidence, failed execution, failed/nonconverged optimization and unavailable targets prevent ranking. A plugin's reported accuracy is never consumed. Point-trajectory and density-ensemble task types are explicitly unsupported by this quantum state evaluator and must use their own independent evaluation routes.

Separate diagnostics retain continuous-reference uncertainty, the normalized discrete-target discrepancy, circuit-output discrepancy and measurement intervals. A `discrete_statevector` can be supplied by an independent classical linear solve to compare circuit output with its actual discretized target. The evaluator does not sum these into an unsupported total error, and never infers a certified ODE accuracy claim from empirical components. Classical reference solving belongs to the platform, not to a plugin claiming quantum execution. Ideal-statevector predictions and actual-shot predictions remain distinct evidence rows; an ideal simulation cannot replace a missing shot experiment.

Provenance includes evaluator schema, NumPy/SciPy versions and source labels. Mathematical sources: Nielsen and Chuang, *Quantum Computation and Quantum Information*, section 9.2 (trace distance); Hoeffding (1963), *Probability inequalities for sums of bounded random variables*; Clopper and Pearson (1934), *Biometrika* 26, 404–413. Bit ordering follows Qiskit's least-significant-qubit integer basis convention.
